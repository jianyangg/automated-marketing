## Coverage rules

Scheduled-initiative walkthrough (instruction-level, not runtime enforcement): an owner requests ongoing marketing → inspect/reuse one host-supported task, verify cadence/timezone and saved scope → obtain relevant research and aggregate data, complete a covered action, verify receipt, then record outcomes/next decision. A standing permission for brand replies must remove redundant per-reply approval stops without enabling DMs, standalone posts, spending, personal-identity disclosure or another owner's accounts. Missing tools/locked desktop preserve partial work and a precise resume action; no scheduler is inferred from repository text. A fresh clone inherits the method, never the private task or permission. Cadence and workload limits are design choices, not research-proven acquisition effects.

Quiet-autonomy walkthrough: owner sets private interruption exceptions → recover that policy and actual action scope → routine decisions and non-critical blockers remain local, independent covered work continues, and applicable exceptions escalate. Quiet does not authorize spend or unsupported public action. Proposed improvement cites inspected research, firsthand founder/operator experience or legitimate outcomes with transfer limits; an unverified growth anecdote remains a hypothesis. Public instructions contain no private identity, email or owner-specific exception list.

The separate local-dashboard suite is implemented in `apps/dashboard/test/` and runs with `npm test` (22 cases verified October 15, 2026). It checks read-only overview/source behavior, dated series and chart gaps, source-linked decision summaries, safe Markdown parsing, unknowns/freshness, workspace confinement and local HTTP/session handling. This provides bounded evidence for the overview portion of FR-20 and saved-observation display under FR-17; it does not satisfy the planned provider, transactional-runtime or execution scenarios above. Visual review remains separate.

Every functional requirement must map to at least one scenario above and a release-level artifact. Connector-specific contract tests add actual payload/permission/idempotency evidence; they cannot be replaced by these prose cases. No R2 claim is satisfied by R1 export success.

The acceptance record separates automated invariants, clean-machine tests, provider commissioning, owner visual review, and marketing outcomes. Passing software tests does not establish customer demand. Each real campaign also needs its own observation and decision criteria.
