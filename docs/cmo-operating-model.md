# Repeatable CMO operating model

This is the generic workflow the framework must encode. It applies to a new business with no prior marketing as well as an established business whose existing approach needs to be reconsidered.

## 1. What the CMO owns

The CMO turns business goals and evidence into coordinated marketing decisions and completed work. Responsibilities include customer understanding, positioning, offer/proof clarity, channel selection, campaigns, useful content, destination/lifecycle improvements, distribution, measurement, and learning.

The CMO can prepare product recommendations and implementation briefs. It does not claim product engineering, release, account access, sales results, or customer validation occurred when it only proposed them.

## 2. Operating cycle and durable artifacts

| Step | Questions answered | Durable artifact | Missing-input path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frame | What outcome matters, for whom, by when, within which resources? | Goal and conversion map | Draft goal and essential owner clarification |
| Investigate | What is true about the offer, customers, alternatives, and current journey? | Evidence ledger and claim records | Bounded research/asset/measurement task |
| Diagnose | What most constrains useful progress? | Bottleneck hypothesis and counterevidence | Validation plan; no forced diagnosis |
| Choose | Which next action outranks plausible alternatives? | Opportunity decision with effort/cash/attention | Defer, reduce scope, or wait |
| Position | What relevant promise is credible and distinct? | Strategy revision and message/proof map | Narrow claim; request proof; test alternative |
| Plan | How do offer, core asset, destination, and channels work together? | Campaign brief and deliverable graph | Explicit dependency/blocker |
| Produce | What complete work will run the plan? | Versioned drafts, compositions, scripts, change/export packs | Text-only path, asset request, partial state |
| Authorize | What exactly can be sent, changed, scheduled, or spent? | Owner grant or scoped standing policy | Preserve drafts; no execution |
| Distribute | What actually went out, where, and when? | Per-destination attempt and receipt | Reconcile, manual export, or attention |
| Observe | What happened under a defined measurement window? | Raw observations, corrections, calculations | Delayed/suppressed/unavailable state |
| Decide | Continue, change, stop, or wait—and why? | Learning decision and next review/action | Preserve uncertainty; avoid manufactured winner |

Every artifact links backward to its evidence/decision and forward to its outcomes. A post without a campaign can be an owner-directed ad hoc item, but still needs an objective/context, truth check, and authority before execution. A validation plan is a first-class work item: question/hypothesis, required evidence, secondary method, source criteria, tasks, effort, authority needs, observations, exit condition, and decision. Research-only work records distribution as not applicable rather than inventing a publication step.

Use [autonomous secondary research](research/secondary-research.md) to investigate material external unknowns with Codex's existing tools. The [evidence library](research/evidence-library.md) records methodological findings and transfer limits. Published research, selected public language, brand facts, internal judgments, and actual outcomes remain distinct. No new surveys, interviews, focus groups, recruitment, or synthetic consumer panels are part of this operating model.

## 3. First onboarding

1. Inventory the chosen folder and classify business facts, assets, preferences, historical marketing, and unknowns.
2. Ask only missing facts that materially change the plan; proceed on explicitly labeled reversible assumptions where possible.
3. Establish the offer, intended conversion, availability/market, current evidence, and resource constraints.
4. Research relevant customer problems and alternatives when tools/network permit; clearly separate supplied context from independently verified findings.
5. Propose a short diagnosis and a small number of strategic choices, with counterevidence and proof gaps.
6. Produce one complete campaign or validation plan, including destination, deliverables, owner tasks, and measurement.
7. Save the operating brief. Connect accounts or activate jobs only as separately chosen next steps.

Past assets can be inspected if deliberately supplied. They do not receive favorable treatment because they already exist. Fresh strategy can reject the old audience, proposition, cadence, or visuals while preserving useful product evidence.

## 4. Strategy contract

For substantial decisions, Codex selects a relevant [thinking framework](research/thinking-frameworks.md): problem decomposition for diagnosis, coherent choices for strategy, a customer-journey lens for friction, or structural competition when choosing markets/routes. These are practitioner methods, separate from empirical marketing evidence. Use only what affects a decision and record it in the existing strategy/brief; no mandatory frameworks, survey program, or additional report fleet.

A strategy revision contains:

- Business outcome, conversion path, baseline, and review horizon.
- Candidate audience and situation: problem, trigger, alternatives, objections, evidence, and reachable places.
- Proposition, differentiators, reasons to believe, and claim exclusions.
- Channel choice with audience fit, format/production constraints, and access/cost assumptions.
- Opportunity priorities, expected learning, capacity allocation, and displaced alternatives.
- Experiments, stop conditions, data gaps, and the next decision.

Customer statements, observed behavior, operator preference, and system interpretation are separate fields. Public-language themes support candidate problems and situations, not representative segments. Source-backed alternative/attribute matrices can inform positioning; analyst maps are not measured consumer perceptions. LLM scenario critique cannot substantiate audience demand. A numerical prioritization score is optional and must not suggest precision beyond its inputs. Adopting a replacement strategy records affected campaigns, queued work, and policies; incompatible future execution is held until an explicit retain/revise decision and appropriate authority are recorded.

## 5. Campaign contract and reuse

A campaign connects one objective and audience to a coherent story, offer/core asset, destination, delivery sequence, and evaluation. It can include a guide, product/service demo, landing-page change, email concept, social variants, FAQ, or referral brief.

Reuse preserves lineage: source/version → core asset → adaptations → delivery → outcome. Each adaptation checks its own audience, platform constraints, call to action, and destination. A website update that changes a material fact flags dependent future work; published records remain historical.

Follow [creative development](research/creative-development.md): different strategic premises before refinement, originality and relevance assessed separately, authentic proof, and explicit placement/CTA tradeoffs. Optional [LLM critique](research/llm-evaluation.md) uses fixed criteria, factual gates, order checks, dissent, and unknown calibration; it is not customer validation.

Campaigns need explicit assumptions about production time and owner participation. A plan that depends on photos, a live demo, or a product change lists those dependencies. Record operator capacity in minutes/hours per planning window, already committed tasks, estimated remaining effort, and actual effort when available. Recompute priorities after capacity/dependency changes; an unknown capacity produces a provisional plan, not an invented budget. It does not claim that Codex can create authentic customer evidence on demand.

## 6. Choosing the next action

| Situation | Plausible priority | What prevents a premature conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New local service, no audience/history | Offer clarity, public-language/alternative research, useful service explanation | Geography, capacity, and pricing may still be unknown |
| Prelaunch tool | Secondary problem research, proof prototype, accurate waitlist destination | Gate availability and unreleased feature claims; no inferred demand |
| Good exposure, weak conversion | Destination, proposition, proof, or onboarding experiment | Verify denominator and traffic quality first |
| Good conversion, little qualified exposure | Relevant distribution and useful discoverable content | Do not assume every available network is suitable |
| Existing content becomes inaccurate | Refresh and affected-asset review | Preserve past versions and approval invalidation |
| Repeated inbound objection | Clarify messaging or prepare product/offer change brief | The objection may reflect a product issue, not copy |
| Two brands compete for owner time | Explicit portfolio tradeoff and sequenced plan | Adding a brand does not add capacity |
| Tiny or incomparable results | Wait or design a clearer test | Engagement alone cannot establish acquisition success |

## 7. Cadence and attention

Cadence is an output of strategy, proof availability, channel access, and operator capacity. There is no framework default of daily posts, three brands, or a fixed monthly creative quota.

A preparation policy can maintain a rolling two-week horizon with a bounded ready queue. It can also maintain a research or destination-improvement backlog instead of a post calendar. Daily checks surface exceptions and time-sensitive work; weekly reviews allocate attention; monthly reviews revisit strategy when evidence justifies it. These are configurable operating patterns, not mandatory timers created at installation.

Default reports show the current goal, highest-priority next action, meaningful change, blockers, and decisions required. Detailed source tables and metrics remain accessible on request. Silence on unchanged state is part of the attention budget.

## 8. Learning and self-review

Separate three feedback loops:

1. **Editorial:** what the owner accepts/edits/rejects and why.
2. **Operational:** truth, completion, delivery, costs, and failure rates.
3. **Market:** qualified response, conversions, customer quality, retention, and available contribution evidence.

Before a campaign is presented, challenge factual claims, audience assumptions, proof, channel fit, destination, and measurement. After execution, compare the expected bottleneck with outcomes and record retain/change/stop/wait. Never optimize only for owner approval or impressions.

Classify outcome conclusions as descriptive, attributed, associational, or causally identified. Path tracking is useful without proving incremental revenue. Preserve low-power/inconclusive results, confounders, and missing baselines. New participant studies are not required; existing authorized outcomes can revise decisions and the applicability of earlier research.

Optional specialist sub-agents can challenge customer assumptions, creative quality, or measurement. Their work is bounded to relevant context and a concrete review question. They provide evidence and dissent; they cannot grant authority or create competing publishing queues. The primary Codex workflow reconciles conflicts into one recorded decision. A single-agent path remains fully supported; delegation is not required to use the framework.

## 9. Operating invariants

- A recommendation is not an executed action.
- Prepared, authorized, locally scheduled, provider accepted, and publicly published are distinct.
- No business fact, customer outcome, or permission is invented to keep the pipeline moving.
- A missing tool, locked credential store, or plan limit preserves partial work and a clear resume action.
- No external action follows merely from adding or copying a folder.
- Fresh strategy does not delete external history or revoke unrelated accounts.
