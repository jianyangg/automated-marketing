# Research evidence and design transfers

Reviewed October 13, 2026. This library informs the operating procedures; it does not establish that this framework increases revenue. Published findings, proposed transfers, and local acceptance results are separate. No new surveys, interviews, recruitment, or synthetic consumer panels are required.

The review is a bounded interdisciplinary desk review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Searches covered literature-review methods, public-review bias and mining, advertising creativity, AI ideation, placement effects, language, sharing, causal advertising measurement, and LLM evaluation/simulation. Primary publisher, author, proceedings, and preprint sources were preferred. Counterevidence includes review selection bias, creative convergence, judge bias, weak preference simulation, and noisy measurement. Search coverage, older advertising contexts, abstract-only access, and uneven replication limit confidence. Recheck changeable papers and claims for each decision through the [secondary-research protocol](secondary-research.md).

## E01 — Transparent secondary research

**Source:** Snyder (2019), “Literature review as a research methodology: An overview and guidelines,” *Journal of Business Research*, 104, 333–339. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2019.07.039). Peer reviewed; indexed publisher abstract and extracts inspected, not full text.

**Finding:** Methodological synthesis distinguishes review approaches and emphasizes matching methods to questions and reporting the process transparently.

**Transfer:** Keep a decision question, query/source plan, inclusion/exclusion reasons, countersearch, access limits, and stopping rationale. Call a bounded review what it is.

**Limit:** Method guidance, not an experiment showing marketing lift. This protocol is an adaptation, not certification as a systematic review. **Coverage:** FR-25; T51–53.

## E02 — Contextual public-language analysis

**Source:** Tirunillai & Tellis (2014), “Mining Marketing Meaning from Online Chatter,” *Journal of Marketing Research*, 51(4), 463–479. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.12.0106), [author paper](https://gtellis.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/lda.pdf). Peer reviewed; full paper inspected.

**Finding:** Topic modeling of approximately 350,000 reviews across five markets extracted dimensions, sentiment, and changing brand positions. Attribute meaning depends on context; filtering rare terms can lose emerging issues.

**Transfer:** Trace themes to deduplicated statements, product versions, attribute meanings, and counterexamples; preserve rare consequential observations.

**Limit:** Our transparent coding procedure is not a validated substitute for the paper’s model. A selected review corpus does not measure population demand. **Coverage:** FR-26; T54–56.

## E03 — Reviews are selected evidence

**Source:** Hu, Pavlou & Zhang (2017), “On Self-Selection Biases in Online Product Reviews,” *MIS Quarterly*, 41(2), 449–471. [Publisher abstract](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misq/vol41/iss2/8/). Peer reviewed; abstract inspected.

**Finding:** Analytical and empirical work identifies acquisition and underreporting selection biases; average ratings need not reflect underlying product quality.

**Transfer:** Document who could appear in each source, missing groups, duplicates, and coverage. Report frequencies only within the collected corpus.

**Limit:** An LLM cannot remove selection bias merely by adding personas or rescaling ratings. Abstract-only access limits methodological appraisal. **Coverage:** FR-26; T54, T56.

## E04 — Originality and appropriateness

**Source:** Rosengren, Eisend, Koslow & Dahlen (2020), “A Meta-Analysis of When and How Advertising Creativity Works,” *Journal of Marketing*, 84(6), 39–56. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242920929288). Peer reviewed; available full paper inspected.

**Finding:** The synthesis covers 93 datasets, 67 papers, and 878 effects. Originality and appropriateness both matter to interpreting creative advertising responses.

**Transfer:** Assess novelty separately from relevance, credible proof, product value, and brand connection. A memorable device needs a business reason.

**Limit:** The analyzed datasets contain no sales outcomes. Internal creative judgments cannot predict sales lift. **Coverage:** FR-27; T57–58.

## E05 — Individual quality and collective similarity

**Source:** Doshi & Hauser (2024), “Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content,” *Science Advances*, 10(28), eadn5290. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290), [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.00506v3). Peer reviewed; primary preprint inspected.

**Finding:** A preregistered short-story experiment involved 293 writers and 600 evaluators. GPT-4 ideas improved evaluated individual creativity while increasing similarity across stories.

**Transfer:** Explore different customer situations, promises, and proof mechanisms before polishing wording; compare the portfolio as well as each concept.

**Limit:** Short fiction does not establish marketing outcomes, universal model behavior, an optimal concept quota, or a subagent cure. **Coverage:** FR-27; T57.

## E06 — Ideation convergence is task dependent

**Source:** Anderson, Shah & Kreminski (2024), “Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation,” *Creativity & Cognition*. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01536v2). Conference paper; manuscript inspected.

**Finding:** Across four divergent tasks, 33 analyzed participants using ChatGPT 3.5 produced more detailed but less distinctive ideas between participants than a card-based aid. Within-person homogenization was not significantly different.

**Transfer:** Inspect premise diversity rather than counting variants. Treat a divergence prompt as a proposed intervention, not a proven remedy.

**Limit:** Small, time-limited laboratory study using older model versions; no conversion outcome. **Coverage:** FR-27; T57.

## E07 — Placement and interruption interact

**Source:** Goldfarb & Tucker (2011), “Online Display Advertising: Targeting and Obtrusiveness,” *Marketing Science*, 30(3), 389–404. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1100.0583), [MIT record](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/d923a58e-d29c-4699-9670-d1306f740b53). Peer reviewed; author-uploaded manuscript inspected.

**Finding:** Exposure/control evidence across 2,892 display campaigns suggests contextual matching and visible formats work differently in combination on stated purchase intent.

**Transfer:** Specify reader intent, placement, interruption, format, and next-action burden together.

**Limit:** Exposure was randomized; context and format were not fully randomized. Older paid display and stated intent cannot prescribe a universal organic format. **Coverage:** FR-27; T59.

## E08 — Concrete language in its context

**Source:** Packard & Berger (2021; online 2020), “How Concrete Language Shapes Customer Satisfaction,” *Journal of Consumer Research*, 47(5), 787–806. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaa038). Peer reviewed; publisher abstract and indexed extracts inspected.

**Finding:** Five studies, including customer-service field interactions, associate or experimentally link concreteness with perceived listening and favorable customer responses.

**Transfer:** Replace vague promises with accurate situations, actions, and proof when appropriate. Preserve clarity and truth.

**Limit:** Customer-service effects do not establish a general advertising conversion rule. Abstract/extract access limits appraisal. More detail is not automatically better copy. **Coverage:** FR-27; T58–59.

## E09 — Sharing differs from business value

**Source:** Berger & Milkman (2012), “What Makes Online Content Viral?”, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 49(2), 192–205. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353), [author paper](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf). Peer reviewed; full paper inspected.

**Finding:** A three-month New York Times corpus and experiments connect emotional activation and content characteristics to transmission or intended sharing.

**Transfer:** State a useful reason to save, use, or share each asset, and measure business relevance separately.

**Limit:** Email sharing and intended sharing are not purchases; results vary with setting. No universal viral score or requirement to manufacture emotion follows. **Coverage:** FR-18, FR-27; T59, T64.

## E10 — Attribution is not incrementality

**Source:** Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky (2019), “A Comparison of Approaches to Advertising Measurement,” *Marketing Science*, 38(2), 193–225. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2018.1135), [author manuscript](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/gordon_b/files/fb_comparison.pdf). Peer reviewed; manuscript inspected.

**Finding:** Observational estimates often differed from results of 15 large Facebook advertising experiments despite extensive controls; discrepancies went both ways.

**Transfer:** Label results descriptive, attributed, associational, or causally identified. Record assumptions and confounding changes.

**Limit:** Paid-platform evidence does not quantify bias in a small organic campaign. Tagged conversions remain useful path observations without proving incremental effect. **Coverage:** FR-17–18; T64.

## E11 — Imprecision can survive large samples

**Source:** Lewis & Rao (2015), “The Unfavorable Economics of Measuring the Returns to Advertising,” *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 130(4), 1941–1973. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv023). Peer reviewed; publisher abstract inspected.

**Finding:** Twenty-five large advertising field experiments illustrate how noisy returns can remain even at substantial scale.

**Transfer:** Preserve uncertainty, distinguish unavailable evidence from no effect, and permit wait/inconclusive decisions.

**Limit:** No sample-size rule for this framework follows. It does not require new experiments or make passive measurement useless. Abstract-only access limits appraisal. **Coverage:** FR-18; T64.

## E12 — LLM judging needs sensitivity checks

**Source:** Zheng et al. (2023), “Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena,” *NeurIPS*. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4). Peer-reviewed conference paper; revised primary manuscript inspected.

**Finding:** Strong judge/human agreement in particular chatbot comparisons coexists with position, verbosity, and reasoning failures; agreement depends on tie handling.

**Transfer:** Fix a rubric, compare neutral labels in both orders, allow ties/abstention, and check factual gates separately.

**Limit:** Chatbot preference benchmarks are not consumer-prediction calibration. Do not transport a benchmark percentage into a general accuracy claim. **Coverage:** FR-28; T60–63.

## E13 — A model can favor its own style

**Source:** Panickssery, Bowman & Feng (2024), “LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations,” *NeurIPS*. [Proceedings paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Paper-Conference.pdf). Peer-reviewed conference paper; full paper inspected.

**Finding:** Summary evaluation with GPT-4, GPT-3.5, and Llama-2-7b-chat exhibited self-recognition and self-preference; interventions linked their strength.

**Transfer:** Hide authorship and generation rationale where possible, retain provenance, and disclose correlated same-model critique.

**Limit:** Style can reveal origin despite neutral labels. A fresh context or subagent is not an independent customer panel. **Coverage:** FR-28; T61–62.

## E14 — Favorable simulation evidence has narrow scope

**Source:** Maier et al. (2025), “LLMs Reproduce Human Purchase Intent via Semantic Similarity Elicitation of Likert Ratings.” [Preprint v3, October 27, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08338v3). Working paper; v1 and current v3 inspected; peer-review status not established here.

**Finding:** GPT-4o/Gemini concept elicitation was compared with 57 US personal-care surveys containing 9,300 human responses. Reported correlation attainment is relative to split-half reliability, not buying accuracy.

**Transfer:** Preserve benchmark, elicitation, model, metric, and population when appraising simulation claims.

**Limit:** Reference anchors were manually optimized for these 57 surveys; out-of-domain transfer is unresolved. Stated intent and buying differ. This framework has no equivalent audience calibration and does not generate synthetic purchase percentages. **Coverage:** FR-28; T62.

## E15 — Preference simulation may transport poorly

**Source:** Chen et al. (2026), “Simulated responses from large language models are weak proxies for human economic preferences across 74 countries,” *Nature Human Behaviour*, published October 12. [Publisher paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02611-4). Peer reviewed; article inspected.

**Finding:** Six preferences, 67,201 matched human respondents, and nine model conditions yielded weak individual alignment and limited reproduction of cultural relationships.

**Transfer:** Do not treat plausible personas or model agreement as representative audience evidence.

**Limit:** Economic preferences and an older human benchmark differ from product-concept tasks; this does not disprove every bounded simulation result. The newly published result needs continuing appraisal. **Coverage:** FR-28; T62.
