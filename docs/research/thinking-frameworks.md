# Decision frameworks for marketing work

Reviewed October 13, 2026. These are practitioner frameworks for organizing questions and choices. Their reputation does not establish causal marketing effectiveness. Keep them separate from the empirical [evidence library](evidence-library.md). The adaptations below are local design choices, not validated algorithms or copied consulting deliverables.

Use the smallest method that improves the current decision. Codex selects it; the owner need not name a framework. A simple copy edit needs no strategy exercise. Record the useful analysis in the existing strategy, research, or campaign record, not a new report for each framework.

| Decision need | Useful lens | Minimum useful output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why are results weak? | Structured problem solving and issue trees | Plausible causes, evidence gaps, and the branch worth investigating |
| Who should we serve and why choose us? | Strategy Choice Cascade | Coherent choices, exclusions, required capabilities, and review conditions |
| Where does the customer need help? | Consumer decision journey | A source-backed obstacle and a useful next action at a relevant moment |
| Is this market or route structurally difficult? | Five Forces | A consequential competitive constraint and a practical response |

## F01 — Break down the problem before choosing a tactic

McKinsey's seven-step problem-solving discussion covers problem definition, logical decomposition, prioritization, work planning, analysis, synthesis, and action. It emphasizes iteration and uncertainty. [Primary transcript, September 13, 2019](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/how-to-master-the-seven-step-problem-solving-process)

**Apply:** define one decision and its time/resource boundary. Decompose plausible causes, then prioritize by consequence, ability to change, and evidence needed. Separate observations from hypotheses, specify disconfirming evidence, and use the secondary-research workflow. Revise the working explanation when evidence changes; overlapping causes and missing data remain explicit.

**Example:** few purchases could reflect unqualified visits, an unclear offer, checkout friction, or measurement error. Investigate a consequential, tractable branch before prescribing more posts. This is an illustrative decomposition, not proof of the cause or an exhaustive tree.

**Limit:** expert method guidance, not a controlled estimate of sales lift. Prioritization is judgment; no automatic 80/20 distribution is assumed.

## F02 — Make strategy a coherent set of choices

Roger Martin's Strategy Choice Cascade links aspiration, where to compete, how to win, capabilities, and management systems. [Author's explanation](https://rogerlmartin.com/thought-pillars/strategy)

**Apply:** state the business outcome; chosen customer situation/market/channel; credible reason to choose this offer; required proof, skills, assets, and capacity; and how progress will be reviewed. Check the choices together. A promise requiring unavailable capabilities changes the strategy or becomes an explicit dependency. State what is deferred and why.

**Output:** one concise choice and tradeoff in the existing strategy record, compared with a plausible alternative. Revisit interdependent choices as evidence changes rather than treating them as a fixed sequential checklist.

**Limit:** a strategy lens, not proof of demand or competitive advantage. Superiority, feasibility, price, and outcomes still need evidence; an internal preference cannot supply it.

## F03 — Help at the relevant moment in the customer's journey

McKinsey's 2009 consumer decision journey considers initial consideration, active evaluation, purchase, and postpurchase experience, including return loops. Its published industry research motivates the model; the article does not provide enough detail to establish universal transfer. [Original article](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-consumer-decision-journey)

**Apply:** map the relevant customer's trigger, information sources, alternatives, obstacle, useful answer/proof, next action, and observable outcome. Link evidence or mark assumptions. Customers can skip, repeat, or return to moments; do not force a linear path. Use public sources and existing legitimate observations, without new participant research.

**Example:** a useful comparison page may address evaluation uncertainty, while clearer setup guidance may address postpurchase friction. Neither intervention is automatically the priority.

**Limit:** observed stage sizes and drop-off remain unknown without suitable data. No invented funnel percentages or universal channel allocation. The [2015 update](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-new-consumer-decision-journey) also illustrates that the original model is revisable.

## F04 — Inspect structural competitive pressures when relevant

Porter's Five Forces examines rivalry, entrants, substitutes, supplier power, and buyer power at industry level. [Harvard Institute for Strategy and Competitiveness](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx)

**Apply:** define the relevant market and identify evidence of consequential pressures: competing alternatives, switching costs, easy entry, customer bargaining, or concentrated access to essential inputs/distribution. Treat platform dependence as a contextual adaptation requiring actual terms and alternatives. Link a pressure to a marketing implication: a narrower reachable audience, clearer proof, different route, or a reversible test.

**Limit:** this is not a company feature scorecard or a numerical profitability forecast. Omit a full analysis when it cannot change the decision. Do not expand a marketing task into an unrequested corporate restructuring exercise.

## Use, record, and stop

Before substantial strategy work, choose a relevant lens or explicitly proceed without one. Produce a recommendation, decisive evidence, unresolved assumption, tradeoff, and next action/review trigger. Save only useful conclusions and links in the existing work record. A framework diagram is optional; completed boxes do not count as evidence. Stop when further analysis will not change the near-term decision within its resource boundary. No framework can authorize publication, spending, surveys, recruitment, or synthetic customer validation.
