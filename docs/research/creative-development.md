# Evidence-informed creative development

Use this procedure for a material campaign concept or positioning choice. It is an engineering adaptation of research, not a validated algorithm for predicting market performance. The [evidence library](evidence-library.md) records research context and limits.

## 1. Ground the brief before generating

Use the current business facts, selected customer situation, alternatives, proposition, proof, destination, format, exclusions, and decision objective. Include a few relevant real-source observations, not an enormous undifferentiated context dump. Do not show historical copy as the default template when fresh strategy is requested.

Identify the useful message: what the audience should understand, why it matters now in the stated situation, why this business can credibly help, and the next action. Product truth and public customer language constrain claims; they do not dictate one generic wording.

## 2. Diverge by mechanism

Before writing polished copy, generate concept briefs under distinct constraints. A default small working set can include:

- A concrete use situation and the obstacle removed.
- A product/service demonstration with visible proof.
- A contrast with a real alternative or doing nothing, acknowledging tradeoffs.
- A useful explanation, diagnostic question, or misconception correction supported by evidence.

These are optional ideation lenses, not scientifically optimal quotas. Select only lenses appropriate to the business; propose a different mechanism when these converge. Distinct persona names, tone changes, or synonym substitutions do not establish distinct ideas.

Record each concept's situation, claim, proof, format, next action, and evidence. Produce divergent briefs before comparing and refining a favorite. Use separate context when available and justified, but do not call repeated roles on one model independent creativity or consumer research.

## 3. Check distinctiveness and relevance separately

The creativity literature supports considering originality together with relevance/appropriateness. A surprising idea with no credible connection to the offer can fail the task. AI ideation research also motivates checking similarity across the concept set; it does not prove a particular diversity prompt eliminates convergence.

Compare concepts for shared claim, proof, scenario, visual structure, and call to action. Merge paraphrases. Compare against a source-linked category/competitor matrix so “different” is not asserted solely from model intuition. Avoid copying a competitor's phrasing, assets, or distinctive execution.

Maintain a simple baseline grounded in the same facts. Keep a candidate that addresses a different plausible mechanism even if an internal judge favors a conventional option. Log the tradeoff; no concept is guaranteed distinctive to actual customers without relevant evidence.

## 4. Develop a useful execution

Write concrete, comprehensible language tied to verified product/service behavior. The concrete-language evidence is strongest in customer-service settings; applying it to an advertisement is a design hypothesis, not a universal conversion rule.

Choose format and emotional tone for the purpose and context. Practical utility or appropriate interest can help motivate sharing, but sharing is not acquisition and the evidence is channel/context dependent. Do not manufacture anger, fear, urgency, fake social proof, or hidden persuasion to pursue attention.

Ensure brand/offer recognition, credible proof, and a truthful next action survive the creative treatment. A clever asset that hides what is offered can be rejected despite a high novelty score. Contextual fit matters; no one appeal, length, posting time, or emotional formula is universally best.

## 5. Evaluate, revise, preserve

Run deterministic truth/rights/format/destination checks, then the [LLM critique protocol](llm-evaluation.md) for structured internal comparison. Preserve candidates, rubric, disagreements, and revision reasons. New content versions invalidate affected execution authority as already specified.

The outcome is a reasoned shortlist or an inconclusive comparison with concrete revision tasks. It is not a synthetic vote, predicted sales result, or automated permission to publish. Use available real-world observations later to revise the choice without claiming causal lift from a before/after graph.
