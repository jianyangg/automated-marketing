# Diagnose and choose marketing strategy

## Inputs

Business goal, current facts/evidence, conversion path, existing work, and resource constraints. Strategy is a decision to test, not a customer finding by itself.

For a substantial decision, consult `docs/research/thinking-frameworks.md` and choose the smallest useful lens: break down possible causes, align strategic choices, locate a customer-journey obstacle, or inspect a consequential competitive pressure. Skip formal analysis when it cannot change the next action. Keep useful conclusions in the existing strategy/brief; no mandatory framework reports.

## Procedure

1. Define the intended outcome, observable conversion, planning horizon, baseline or missing baseline, and constraints.
2. Inspect offer readiness, customer problem/trigger, reachable audience, alternatives, proof, destination, acquisition, activation, and lifecycle where relevant.
   Use `prompts/research.md` to investigate material external unknowns. Build a source-backed alternative/attribute matrix; label any visual positioning map as analyst interpretation. Public-language themes are candidate problems, not representative customer segments or measured preferences.
3. State the most plausible bottleneck and contrary evidence. If evidence is insufficient, propose a validation plan rather than a confident diagnosis.
   For a causal diagnosis, separate observations from plausible cause branches, identify what would disconfirm the leading hypothesis, and prioritize an important branch the business can influence. A tidy tree is not proof that all causes were found.
4. Compare useful actions: research, proposition/offer clarity, destination repair, creation, refresh/reuse, distribution, inbound/lifecycle, or waiting.
5. Rank a small set by expected learning/outcome, evidence/confidence, operator effort, cash, dependencies, and reversibility. Use planning-window minutes/hours, committed tasks, and estimated remaining effort; unknown capacity makes the plan provisional.
6. Propose audience/situation, proposition, reasons to believe, exclusions, channel rationale, core work, measurement, and stop conditions. Explain the choice against at least one plausible alternative.
   Check that chosen audience, promise, capabilities/proof, resources, and review method fit together. Link the selected work to a relevant customer moment and obstacle; returning/nonlinear paths and unknown stage sizes stay explicit. Use structural-competition analysis only if it changes market/channel choices.
7. On adoption/supersession, identify affected campaigns, queued work, and policies. Hold incompatible future execution until a retain/revise decision and applicable authority are recorded.

## Deliverables

One concise strategy decision with evidence, uncertainty, resource tradeoff, next work item, and review condition; detailed source/evidence records remain linked.

## Review

Challenge whether the plan merely increases content, confuses owner preference with demand, neglects destination/offer quality, assumes a channel audience, or claims numerical precision without support.
